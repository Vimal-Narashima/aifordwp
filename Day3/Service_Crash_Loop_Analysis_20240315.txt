Service Crash Loop Incident Analysis
Date of events: 2024-03-15
Source: Service Control Manager (System log)
Service: Print Spooler

What each Event ID records

7034
- Records that a service terminated unexpectedly.
- In this case, Print Spooler crashed repeatedly, with the crash count incrementing.

7031
- Records unexpected service termination and the configured recovery action.
- Here, after the 4th crash, Service Control Manager planned to restart the service after 60000 milliseconds.

7023
- Records that a service terminated with a specific service-reported error.
- Here, the error is: "The specified module could not be found."

7038
- Records a service logon failure for the configured service account.
- Here, Print Spooler could not log on as NT AUTHORITY\SYSTEM because the required logon type was not granted on the computer.

Sequence of events in plain English

1) At 10:01:14, Print Spooler crashed for the first time (7034).
2) It restarted and crashed again at 10:01:45 (7034, second crash).
3) It restarted and crashed again at 10:02:16 (7034, third crash).
4) At 10:02:47, it crashed a fourth time; SCM stated it would restart the service in 60000 ms (7031).
5) At 10:03:49, after the restart cycle, Spooler terminated with "The specified module could not be found" (7023).
6) At 10:03:50, another start attempt failed with a logon-rights error for NT AUTHORITY\SYSTEM (7038).

Most likely cause with evidence

Primary likely cause:
- Missing or corrupted Spooler-related module (for example, a print driver/print processor DLL or dependent component).
- Evidence: Event 7023 explicitly states "The specified module could not be found."
- Supporting pattern: repeated 7034 events indicate ongoing instability and crash-loop behavior.

Secondary/compounding issue:
- Service account logon rights misconfiguration or policy conflict.
- Evidence: Event 7038 states the service account was not granted the requested logon type.
- Impact: this would block or further disrupt recovery restart attempts.

Conclusion:
- The incident is most consistent with a Print Spooler crash loop triggered by a missing module, with service logon-rights failure compounding recovery failure.
