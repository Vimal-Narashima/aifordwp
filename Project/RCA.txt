Date of RCA: 2026-08-14
Prepared for: DWP service desk and L2 support handoff

The applied resolution restored service, and post-remediation verification confirmed successful user logon to host with no further issues reported.

5) Survived hypothesis (final)
Final surviving hypothesis:
A Windows 11 / Intune sign-in-policy or profile-state issue in the shared migrated configuration path caused slow/failed logon for multiple Floor 6 users.

Why this hypothesis survived elimination
- Multi-user simultaneous impact aligns with a shared configuration or identity path issue.
- Timing after migration/change window supports a change-related service degradation scenario.
- Resolution targeting shared sign-in/policy/profile path restored service and passed verification.

6) Root cause statement
Probable root cause:
An endpoint management/sign-in configuration drift or misapplied policy/profile state in the recent Win11/Intune migrated baseline introduced a floor-wide logon performance/failure condition.

Confirmed status
- Service restored: Yes
- Verification passed: Yes (user can log in to host; no issue reported)
- Exact misconfigured setting/value: to confirm via change log and policy diff review

4. Contained impact by pausing/rolling back the suspected sign-in/policy/profile change (change artifact ID to confirm).

9) 5-Whys analysis
Problem statement: Users on Floor 6 could not log in or experienced severe logon delay.

Why 1: Why were users unable to log in promptly?
- Because the logon path on impacted devices was degraded/failing in the shared migrated environment.

Why 2: Why was the shared logon path degraded?
- Because sign-in policy/profile state for the migrated floor cohort was likely misapplied or drifted (exact setting to confirm).

Why 3: Why could misapplied/drifted policy affect many users at once?
- Because the change targeted a common management baseline (Win11 + Intune cohort), not a single endpoint.

Why 4: Why was this not prevented before floor-wide impact?
- Because pre-production/staged validation and post-change guard checks for sign-in health were not sufficiently enforced for this cohort (control maturity to confirm).

Why 5: Why were controls insufficient?
- Because rollout governance did not fully require cohort-specific sign-in health gates, rollback triggers, and rapid anomaly detection before business start.

Root of roots:
- Change governance and validation controls around shared sign-in/policy baselines were not robust enough to prevent or detect the issue before user impact.

10) Preventive actions
Priority 0 (immediate)
- Enforce staged rollout for sign-in/profile/identity-related Intune and Win11 policy changes.
- Require explicit go/no-go sign-in health checks before broad deployment.

Priority 1 (short term)
- Add rollback criteria and threshold triggers (for example, login failure rate and login duration anomalies) for the first cohort.
- Introduce pre-business-hour health validation for high-impact floors after change windows.

Priority 2 (medium term)
- Build baseline compliance checks to detect policy drift in sign-in/profile settings.
- Create a standard evidence pack template for floor-wide login incidents (event logs, policy diff, cohort device sample, verification output).
